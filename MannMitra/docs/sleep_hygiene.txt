# Sleep Hygiene for College Students
Quality of sleep matters more than quantity. Even if you have limited hours,
these evidence-based strategies can help you fall asleep faster and wake up
more refreshed.

# Power Napping Strategy
A 20-minute power nap between 1-3 PM can restore alertness equivalent to
an extra hour of nighttime sleep. Set an alarm so you don't drift into deep
sleep. Napping in a quiet, dimly lit spot between classes can significantly
boost your concentration for evening study sessions.

# 4-7-8 Sleep Onset Technique
Place the tip of your tongue behind your upper front teeth. Inhale through
your nose for 4 seconds. Hold your breath for 7 seconds. Exhale slowly
through your mouth for 8 seconds, making a gentle whoosh. Repeat 3 times.
This activates your parasympathetic nervous system and most students report
falling asleep within 2-3 cycles.

# Pre-Sleep Wind-Down Routine
Stop screen usage 30 minutes before bed. Keep your room cool (around 24°C)
and as dark as possible. If you have a noisy hostel, earplugs or white noise
from a fan can help. Avoid heavy meals and caffeine after 4 PM. A short body
scan meditation or progressive muscle relaxation (tensing and releasing each
muscle group from feet to forehead) can help your body transition into sleep
mode.

# When Sleep Deprivation is Affecting Your Academics
If you consistently sleep less than 5 hours for more than a week and it is
affecting your concentration, attendance, or mood, please reach out to your
campus Student Wellness Center or call Tele-MANAS (14416) for free,
confidential guidance. Sleep deprivation is a health concern and campus
counselors can help you build a sustainable routine.
